Why it sticks when the handbook does not

A kid can skim a chapter. A kid cannot skim a boss fight.

I · Study

The study cards show the rule but hide the key number until your kid guesses. Guessing first, even guessing wrong, makes the real answer stick harder. Researchers call this the pretesting effect. Kids call it flipping a card.

II · Battle

Six bosses per world. Answer right, you strike. Answer wrong, the boss strikes back and taunts you about it. Nobody forgets the question a boss mocked them over.

III · Redeem

Miss a question and it goes in the Mistake Ledger. The Trial of Redemption drills only those misses until each one is cleared. Weak spots do not get left behind. The game will not let them.

IV · Prove It

The mock exam is 25 questions at the same 80 percent pass bar the real DOL test uses. The breakdown names the weakest topic outright, so before test day you know exactly what is shaky and what is solid.

We did not make this up. Researchers did.

In 2013, Dunlosky and colleagues reviewed the ten most common study techniques. Practice testing and distributed practice came out on top. Re-reading and highlighting, the two things most kids actually do, landed near the bottom.

Permit Legends is those two winning techniques wearing a boss costume. The hidden-number cards run on the pretesting effect (Richland, Kornell and Kao, 2009): guess first, remember longer.
